Astronomical alert triage must connect a finite review budget to inspectable evidence without treating a priority score as a discovery probability. SIDEREA is research software for local photometry analysis, candidate evidence records, catalogue-check state and human review. We present its software workflow and an explicit treatment of equal-score candidates at a review-budget boundary.

The benchmark assigns the available positions within a tied group by a declared uniform-selection expectation. It groups equal scores when computing average precision and orders physical-entity identifiers canonically before paired resampling. An exhaustive illustration evaluates all 24 row permutations of a four-object constructed cohort, for two score columns and four review budgets. Across all 192 evaluations, the reported expected precision and recall are invariant and agree with exact rational averages over the corresponding row-order selections. For the all-tied score column, expected precision is one half at every budget; a single-row ordering can instead give zero or one at budgets of one or two. The example illustrates metric semantics, not sky performance or a new learning result.

The software preserves the distinction between ranking, external catalogue evidence and reportability. Missing or failed external checks do not become clearances, and model scores cannot replace human review. The presentation includes the runnable arithmetic example, exact inputs, source hashes and an offline walkthrough. Real-sky completeness, purity, discovery yield and comparative model benefit remain unestablished. Prospective validation with dated photometry, independent labels and the frozen evaluation protocol is separate work.
